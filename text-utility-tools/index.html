What Are Text Utility Tools?

Text utility tools are the workhorses of this site: counters, cleaners, case converters, and text transformers for everyday writing and editing. No styling, no decoration — just practical operations like counting words, stripping messy formatting, converting case, removing duplicate lines, and fixing broken paragraphs. Students, writers, developers, and social media managers use them daily.

Like everything here, they run entirely in your browser. Paste your text, get the result instantly, and nothing is uploaded or stored — which also makes them safe for drafts you would rather not send to a server.

Who Uses These Tools

  • Students check essay word counts, fix pasted quotes with broken line breaks, and convert headings to title case.
  • Writers and editors clean manuscripts, dedupe lists, and tighten paragraphs to length.
  • Developers convert variable names between camelCase, PascalCase, snake_case, and kebab-case.
  • Data cleaners remove duplicate lines from exported lists and normalize messy copied text.

Guide 1: Hitting a Word Count

Working to a 500-word essay or a 150-word product blurb? Paste your draft into the word counter to see words, characters, and reading time. Over the limit: run long sections through the paragraph shortener. Under it: develop thin sentences with the sentence expander. Iterate until the count lands where you need it.

Guide 2: Cleaning Messy Copied Text

Text copied from PDFs and websites arrives with double spaces, stray line breaks, and phantom characters. Run it through the text cleaner first to normalize whitespace, then the line break fixer to rebuild proper paragraphs. If you pasted a list with repeats — common with exported data — finish with remove duplicate lines. Three steps, one clean document.

Guide 3: Case Conversion for Code and Content

The CamelCase converter switches identifiers between camelCase, PascalCase, snake_case, and kebab-case for JavaScript, Python, CSS, and URLs. For prose, the title case converter fixes headlines, and the lowercase and uppercase converters normalize text in one click.

How Word Counting Works

The word counter splits your text on whitespace and punctuation boundaries — the same method most word processors use — so its count matches Google Docs and Word in most cases. Reading time assumes roughly 200–250 words per minute.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I count words in my text?

Paste your text into the word counter. It instantly reports words, characters (with and without spaces), sentences, paragraphs, and estimated reading time. There is no word limit, and your text never leaves your browser.

How do I remove duplicate lines from a list?

Paste the list into the remove duplicate lines tool. It scans every line, keeps the first occurrence of each unique line, and deletes the repeats — leaving a clean deduplicated list you can copy back out.

How do I fix broken line breaks from copied text?

PDFs and web pages often paste with a line break after every visual line. The line break fixer joins those fragments back into proper paragraphs, and the text cleaner removes the leftover double spaces and stray characters in the same pass.

What is zalgo text?

Zalgo text is the creepy, glitchy-looking text made by stacking combining diacritical marks — the Unicode characters normally used for accents — above and below each letter. The zalgo text generator does this automatically; use it sparingly, since heavy zalgo can break layouts and annoy screen readers.

How do I make spoiler text on Discord?

Discord hides text wrapped in double bars: ||like this||. The spoiler text generator formats your text that way automatically — it also works on Reddit with its own spoiler markup — so readers click to reveal instead of being spoiled.

Are these text utility tools free?

Yes — all 15 are free, unlimited, and private. Processing happens in your browser, so even sensitive drafts are never uploaded anywhere.
