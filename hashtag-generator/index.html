What Is a Hashtag Generator?

A hashtag generator is an online tool that creates sets of hashtags matched to your content topic. You enter what the post is about; the tool returns grouped hashtags — broad popular tags plus niche categories like travel, food, or fitness — and even invents custom tags from your topic words (for example, “travel” becomes #travellife, #travelgram, #travelvibes). You tap the tags you want and copy the selection. It replaces the ritual of retyping the same 30 tags — or worse, guessing at them — every time you post.

This hashtag maker uses a curated library, not live trend data: 120 hand-picked tags across 8 categories. That means the tags are stable and relevant rather than chasing this week’s spikes — a deliberate tradeoff covered in the limitations section.

How This Tool Works

  1. Topic input. Type your content topic (“travel,” “skincare,” “home workout”). The tool lowercases and trims it for matching.
  2. Popular tags always show. The 🔥 Popular category (15 tags: #fyp, #viral, #trending, #explorepage, #instagood, #photooftheday …) appears every time as a starting pool.
  3. Smart category matching. If your topic matches a category name or any tag inside it — “travel” matches the travel category, “yoga” matches tags inside fitness — that category’s 15 tags are shown too.
  4. Custom tags from your words. Your topic also generates 10 custom hashtags: the topic itself plus variants like topic + s, topic + life, topic + lover, insta + topic, topic + gram, topic + photography, topic + vibes, topic + goals, and my + topic (filtered to 30 characters max).
  5. Select and copy. Click any tag to toggle it into your selection, then copy the chosen set — exactly the tags you want, formatted with # and spaced, ready to paste.
  6. No topic? Leaving the box empty shows all 8 categories, so you can browse and hand-pick.

The 3–5 Tag Strategy (Why Fewer Beats 30)

Instagram allows 30 hashtags per post, which tempts everyone to use 30. The problem: tags like #love (billions of posts) bury you in seconds, while 30 tags dilute the signal about what your post is actually about. The structure that works for most accounts is 3–5 tags in descending specificity: one broad tag for volume (#travel), two community tags where you can rank (#travelblogger, #travelphotography), and one custom or branded tag (#travellife). Each tag should describe the post so accurately that a stranger clicking it finds exactly what they expected — relevance is the ranking factor you control.

Rotate, don’t repeat identically. Posting the same 30 tags on every post looks automated and wastes the chance to test. Keep 2–3 anchor tags constant for series consistency and swap the rest per post using this generator — that is what the custom-tag section is for: #myparisguide on this post, #myitalyguide on the next.

Finding Tags That Actually Fit Your Niche

The matching logic here rewards specific topics: “travel” surfaces the travel category, but “vegan” matches tags inside food, and a topic like “skincare” hits beauty. Try your niche three ways — the broad term, the community term (“bookstagram” not “books”), and the activity (“mealprep”) — and compare which returns the most usable tags. Then sanity-check tag sizes in the Instagram app before committing: the sweet spot for small and mid-size accounts is tags with roughly 50K–500K posts, big enough to have an audience, small enough that your post can surface in Top posts. This generator gives you the words; the app gives you the sizes; together they make the set.

Limitations

  • Curated library, not live trends. The 120 tags are hand-picked and stable — the tool will not surface this week’s viral tag or warn you that a tag is banned or saturated. Check tag health in the app for important posts.
  • English tags only. All categories and custom patterns are English-language.
  • Custom tags are formulaic. Patterns like insta + topic produce real-looking tags (#instatravel) but also duds — eyeball each custom tag before using it.
  • No performance data. The tool cannot tell you which tags drove reach; track that in Instagram Insights or TikTok analytics.
  • Matching is keyword-based. Unusual topics may match no category beyond Popular — in that case the custom tags carry the load.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the best Instagram hashtags?

The best Instagram hashtags are specific to your post and sized to your account: 3–5 tags mixing one broad, two community (50K–500K posts), and one custom tag. Enter your topic above to build such a set — relevance beats raw popularity every time.

How to find trending hashtags?

This tool provides stable, curated tags rather than live trend data. For genuinely trending tags, check TikTok’s Discover page or Instagram’s search suggestions for your niche — then combine those with the evergreen sets generated here for a balanced mix.

Is there a hashtag generator for Reels?

Yes — this one. Reels use the same hashtag system as feed posts, so generate a 3–5 tag set above and paste it after your Reel caption. For TikTok videos, the TikTok caption generator bakes trend tags into captions directly.

How many hashtags should I use per post?

Instagram allows up to 30, but 3–5 highly relevant tags generally perform better — you can actually rank in mid-size tags instead of drowning in giant ones. Select your 3–5 above with click-to-select and copy just those.

Where do I put hashtags — caption or comments?

In the caption, after the text — separated by a few line breaks or a divider so the feed view stays clean. Hashtags in comments work but are less reliable for discovery and harder for you to manage.

Can hashtags get my account shadowbanned?

Using banned or spam-associated tags can limit a post’s reach, which people call shadowbanning. Stick to relevant, legitimate tags like the curated ones here, avoid repeating identical 30-tag blocks on every post, and check any unfamiliar tag in Instagram search before using it.

Do hashtags still work in 2026?

Yes, but their role narrowed: keyword-rich captions and the algorithm’s own categorization do more discovery work than before. Hashtags now function best as precise categorization — a few accurate tags beat a wall of hopeful ones.

Can I copy the hashtags directly?

Yes — click tags to select them, then hit Copy Selected. The copied text is formatted with # symbols and spaces, ready to paste into Instagram or TikTok.

Is the hashtag generator free?

Completely free, no account, no limits — generate, select, and copy as many sets as you like. Your topics never leave your browser.
