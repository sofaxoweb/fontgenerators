YouTube Title Generator

By FontGenio Editorial Team · Last updated: October 12, 2026

A YouTube title generator is a free tool that creates catchy video titles from your topic and video type. Enter what the video is about — “sourdough bread,” “iPhone 17 review,” “Tokyo vlog” — pick one of 8 formats like how-to, review, or vlog, and get 6 catchy titles for videos instantly. No sign-up required.

Key takeaways

  • Produces 6 titles per click from 64 templates across 8 video types.
  • Types cover how-to, review, vlog, listicle, challenge, reaction, storytime, and comparison.
  • Titles use proven patterns: numbers, brackets, curiosity gaps, and outcome promises.
  • Every title shows its character count against YouTube’s 100-character title limit.

What Is a YouTube Title Generator?

A YouTube title generator is an online tool that writes title ideas for YouTube videos. You enter the video topic and choose the video format; the tool fills proven title templates with your topic and returns several options to copy. Because the title and thumbnail decide whether anyone clicks, having six varied options to choose from beats drafting alone — especially for creators publishing on a schedule.

This video title maker is template-based rather than AI: 8 templates per video type, shuffled into 6 results per click. It is fast, free, and honest about what it is — a brainstorming partner, not a ranking guarantee. Pair a strong title with a matching thumbnail and the click-through rate takes care of itself.

How This Tool Works

  1. Video topic input. Type the subject in a few words. The topic is inserted verbatim into each title template.
  2. Video type selection. Eight buttons — how-to, review, vlog, listicle, challenge, reaction, storytime, comparison — set the title formula. How-to is the default.
  3. Template fill. Each type holds 8 title templates with a {topic} placeholder. The tool shuffles them and returns 6, substituting your topic.
  4. Character count. Each title displays its length, so you can keep it under YouTube’s 100-character title limit — and ideally under ~60 characters, where search results truncate.
  5. One-click copy. Copy any title straight to your clipboard for pasting into YouTube Studio.

Clicking generate again reshuffles the templates for a fresh set of six.

These are verbatim template outputs with the topic substituted — note the classic title patterns at work: the beginner promise, the pipe-separated keyword format, and the superlative-plus-year formula. Capitalize the topic yourself where the template left it lowercase; the tool pastes exactly what you typed.

How to Use This YouTube Title Tool

  1. Enter your video topic exactly as viewers would search it (budget skincare routine, not my routine!!).
  2. Choose the video type that matches your format — listicle for roundups, comparison for versus videos.
  3. Click Generate Titles and shortlist the two strongest.
  4. Fix capitalization and trim to under ~60 characters so the full title shows in search.
  5. Copy the winner into YouTube Studio, then design a thumbnail that completes (not repeats) the title.

Front-Load the Keyword, Back-Load the Curiosity

YouTube titles serve two audiences: the search algorithm and the human skimming results. The structure that satisfies both is keyword first, curiosity second — “Sourdough Bread Tutorial | Step by Step Guide” rather than “You Won’t Believe What I Baked Today.” Search only weighs the first ~60 characters heavily, and viewers decide in about a second, so the topic must be instantly recognizable. Use this generator’s how-to, review, and comparison types for search-driven videos, and save the curiosity-gap templates (challenge, reaction, storytime) — the classic viral YouTube titles formulas — for browse-driven videos where the thumbnail carries the context.

One more practical note: numbers outperform adjectives. “7 Sourdough Mistakes” beats “Amazing Sourdough Tips” because it promises a countable payoff. When a generated title lacks a number, adding one is the single highest-value edit — it takes five seconds and measurably lifts click-through.

Clickbait vs. Curiosity: Where the Line Is

“Clickbait titles” get searched a lot, but the kind that works long-term is curiosity with a payoff, not deception. A title like “The EASIEST Way to Sourdough Bread” is a promise the video must keep in the first 60 seconds — if it does, watch time rewards you; if it does not, viewers leave and the algorithm notices. The rule: every curiosity gap in the title needs a timestamp in the video where it closes. Generate freely, but before publishing, ask of each title: “does my video deliver this in the first minute?” If not, pick a humbler template.

Limitations

  • No search data. The tool does not know keyword volume or competition — it writes titles, it does not do keyword research.
  • Template patterns repeat. With 8 templates per type, heavy users will recognize the formulas; rotate types and edit outputs.
  • Capitalization is yours to fix. Topics paste in exactly as typed, so “sourdough bread” stays lowercase unless you capitalize it.
  • Some templates carry old years. A template may include a year like (2024) — update it to the current year before publishing.
  • English only. All templates are written in English.

Frequently Asked Questions

How to write YouTube titles?

Put the searchable keyword first, add a curiosity or benefit element second, and keep it under ~60 characters so it does not truncate in search results. Numbers, brackets, and outcome promises (“in 10 minutes,” “for beginners”) consistently lift clicks.

What are the best YouTube title ideas?

The best titles match the video’s intent: how-tos promise an outcome, reviews promise a verdict, listicles promise a countable set. Generate six options above with the matching video type, then pick the one you would click yourself — that gut check is surprisingly reliable.

What makes a catchy title for a video?

Catchy titles combine specificity with an open loop: a concrete subject plus an unresolved promise. “7 Sourdough Mistakes Beginners Make” names the payoff (7 mistakes) while leaving the details for the click. Vague superlatives (“amazing,” “incredible”) without specifics underperform.

How long can a YouTube title be?

YouTube allows 100 characters per title, but search results truncate around 60. This generator shows each title’s character count — aim for the full idea in under 60 characters, and verify with the caption length counter.

Do clickbait titles actually work?

Curiosity-gap titles raise click-through, but only pay off if the video delivers the promised payoff quickly. Misleading titles spike clicks and then kill watch time, which hurts the video overall. Promise boldly, deliver in the first minute.

Should the title match the thumbnail?

They should complement, not duplicate. If the thumbnail shows the before/after, the title can carry the keyword and the hook — repeating the same words in both wastes half your packaging. Pick the title first, then design the thumbnail around what the title does not say.

Can I use these titles for TikTok or Reels?

YouTube titles are too long-form for TikTok captions, which favor short hooks. For short video, use the TikTok caption generator or the Instagram caption generator instead.

Do I need an account to generate titles?

No. The tool runs entirely in your browser with no sign-up and no data collection.

Why do generated titles sometimes repeat?

Each video type has 8 templates and you see 6 per click, so repeats happen. Try a different video type for fresh formulas, or take a favorite and rewrite it with your own specifics — that usually beats regenerating.
