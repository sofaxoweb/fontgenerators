What Is Title Case?

Title case is a capitalization style where the first letter of most words in a title, heading, or headline is capitalized. However, the rules vary by style guide. In AP Style, prepositions of four or more letters are capitalized. In Chicago Style, short prepositions (in, on, at) are lowercase. In APA Style, only the first word and proper nouns are capitalized. This tool supports all major formats so you can match your publication's specific requirements without memorizing every rule.

Why Title Case Matters

Inconsistent capitalization undermines credibility. A blog headline that reads "the best ways to lose weight fast" looks amateurish compared to "The Best Ways to Lose Weight Fast." For SEO, properly capitalized headlines in title tags and H1 tags signal quality to both readers and search engines. Publications and content agencies maintain strict style guides specifically to ensure consistency — and this tool automates that compliance so editors can focus on ideas rather than proofreading capitalization.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the most commonly used title case style?

AP Style is standard for journalism and most US publications. Chicago Style is preferred for books and academic publishing. APA is used in psychology and social science research. For SEO content, AP or Chicago are most common.

Should I use title case for subheadings (H2, H3)?

It depends on your style guide. Most content marketers use sentence case (only capitalize the first word) for H2 and H3 subheadings, reserving full title case for H1 headlines only.

Does the tool handle hyphenated words?

Yes. Hyphenated words like 'well-being' are handled according to each style guide's rules — generally both parts are capitalized (Well-Being) in title case.

What about all-caps acronyms in titles?

Acronyms like NASA, SEO, and HTML stay in all-caps regardless of their position in the title. The converter recognizes common acronyms automatically.

Is there a difference between title case and headline case?

They're the same thing — both refer to capitalizing the principal words in a heading. 'Headline case' is more common in journalism; 'title case' is the broader academic term.
