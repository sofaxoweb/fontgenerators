What is kaomoji?

Definition: Kaomoji (顔文字, literally "face characters") are Japanese text emoticons made from keyboard characters — punctuation, katakana, and symbols — arranged as faces you read without tilting your head, such as (◕‿◕) or (╯°□°）╯.

Kaomoji grew out of Japanese ASCII-art culture on early bulletin boards in the 1980s and spread worldwide with anime and gaming communities. Fans also call them kawaii emoticons. Because they use ordinary characters instead of picture glyphs, a kaomoji never changes appearance between an iPhone and a PC — the face you copy is the face everyone sees.

How this tool works

The library holds 190 hand-listed kaomoji, each tagged with a category and a mood label (e.g. "Joyful", "Sobbing", "Shrug"). Category tabs filter the grid; the search box matches your keyword against face names and labels. Clicking a face writes its exact characters to your clipboard via the browser's clipboard API — the tool never alters the face, so complex multi-code-point faces paste perfectly.

The faces draw on several Unicode blocks: CJK Symbols and Punctuation for fullwidth brackets （）［］, Katakana for ・ ッ and ｀, Hiragana, and the Miscellaneous Symbols and Geometric Shapes blocks for eyes and mouths (◕ ‿ ◡ ≧). This is also why kaomoji need a font with decent CJK coverage — on systems without it, a face can degrade into boxes.

Where kaomoji shine

  • Discord: kaomoji for Discord are a staple — they work in chat, nicknames, and status text without needing Nitro or custom emoji slots.
  • Plain-text contexts: emails, code comments, terminal chats, and old forums where color emojis may not render.
  • Bios and usernames: fullwidth characters stand out in display names on X, TikTok, and Instagram.
  • Lenny-style reactions: the Actions category holds table flips, shrugs, and celebratory faces (e.g. ＼(＾▽＾)／) that emojis cannot replicate.

Limitations

  • Needs CJK font support. Faces using katakana or fullwidth brackets show as boxes on systems without East Asian fonts — rare on modern phones, possible on minimal Linux installs.
  • Width can break layouts. Fullwidth characters are twice as wide as ASCII; long faces can wrap awkwardly in narrow chat bubbles.
  • Screen readers spell them out. Assistive tech may read (╥﹏╥) character by character — avoid kaomoji in essential content.
  • Fixed library. The tool offers 190 curated faces; it does not generate new ones.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is kaomoji?

Kaomoji are Japanese text emoticons — faces built from punctuation and Japanese characters, like (◕‿◕). Unlike Western emoticons, you read them straight-on without tilting your head. They originated in 1980s Japanese online culture.

How do I copy and paste kaomoji?

Click any face in the grid and it is copied to your clipboard automatically. Then paste it into Discord, WhatsApp, a forum, or any text field. The faces are plain text, so they work everywhere.

What is the difference between kaomoji and emoticons?

Kaomoji are read horizontally without tilting your head and use Japanese characters (◕‿◕). Western emoticons like :-) are read sideways and use ASCII only. Kaomoji are generally more detailed and expressive. See the emoticon maker for the classic sideways style.

Can I use kaomoji on Discord?

Yes — kaomoji for Discord work in messages, nicknames, and channel descriptions with no special setup. Because they are text, they do not use emoji slots and display for everyone, including users without Nitro.

Do kaomoji work on iPhone and Android?

Yes. Modern iOS and Android ship fonts with full CJK coverage, so kaomoji render correctly. On very old devices or minimal desktop Linux installs without East Asian fonts, some faces may show as boxes.

How many kaomoji are in the generator?

190 faces across 10 categories: Happy, Love, Sad, Angry, Surprised, Cute, Shrug, Animals, Actions, and Misc. Use the search box to filter by mood, e.g. "cry" or "love".

What does (◕‿◕) mean?

It is a content, pleased face — the ◕ ◕ are smiling eyes and ‿ is a gentle smile. It is one of the most widely recognized cute kaomoji and works as a friendly all-purpose reaction.

Why is my kaomoji showing as boxes?

Boxes mean the device lacks a font covering those characters — usually katakana or fullwidth punctuation. Installing an East Asian language pack or updating the OS resolves it on virtually all systems.

Can I use kaomoji in my username?

In display names on most platforms (X, TikTok, Instagram, Discord), yes. Actual account handles/usernames are usually restricted to ASCII letters, numbers, and underscores, so kaomoji will not work there.
